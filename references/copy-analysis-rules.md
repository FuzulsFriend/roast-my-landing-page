# Copywriting Audit Framework

Rules and checks for evaluating landing page copy quality. Every rule includes what to look for, what good looks like, and what to flag.

---

## Headline Rules

The headline is the single most important piece of copy on the page. 80% of visitors read the headline; only 20% read the rest.

### Rule 1: Specific beats vague
- **Good:** "Cut your meeting time in half with async video updates"
- **Bad:** "Transform the way your team communicates"
- **Test:** Can you picture the outcome? If it could apply to 100 different products, it's too vague.

### Rule 2: The "so what?" test
- **Method:** Read the headline and ask "so what?" If you can't immediately answer why the visitor should care, the headline fails.
- **Good:** "Send invoices in 30 seconds. Get paid 2x faster." (So what? I get paid faster.)
- **Bad:** "The future of invoicing is here." (So what? What does that mean for me?)

### Rule 3: One idea per headline
- **Flag:** Headlines that try to communicate two benefits: "Save time AND money with our all-in-one platform"
- **Better:** Pick the stronger benefit. Use the subhead for the second.

### Rule 4: Avoid "clever" over "clear"
- **Flag:** Puns, wordplay, or cultural references that require insider knowledge
- **Bad:** "Don't just wing it" (for a restaurant management tool — too clever)
- **Good:** "Run your restaurant from your phone" (clear, immediate understanding)

### Rule 5: Under 12 words
- **Flag:** Headlines over 12 words. They're not scannable at a glance.
- **Exception:** SaaS products with technical audiences may need 15 words to be specific enough.

### Rule 6: Avoid starting with "We"
- **Flag:** "We help businesses..." or "We built the first..."
- **Better:** "Your business gets..." or "The first... for [audience]"

## Subheadline Rules

The subhead expands on the headline. Together, headline + subhead should give a complete picture.

### Rule 1: Expand, don't repeat
- **Flag:** Subhead that restates the headline in slightly different words
- **Good:** Headline says what. Subhead says how, for whom, or how fast.

### Rule 2: Add the missing context
- **If headline is benefit-focused**, subhead can explain the mechanism: "AI-powered scheduling that learns your preferences"
- **If headline names the audience**, subhead states the benefit: "Reduce response time from hours to minutes"

### Rule 3: Keep it under 25 words
- **Flag:** Subheads that run to 3 lines. They should be scannable, not paragraphs.

### Rule 4: No jargon in the subhead
- The subhead is still above the fold. It needs to be immediately clear to any visitor.

## Body Copy Rules

Everything below the fold. This is where trust is built and objections are handled.

### Rule 1: Short paragraphs
- **Maximum:** 3 lines per paragraph on desktop (roughly 2 sentences)
- **Flag:** Any paragraph over 4 lines — visitors skip these entirely
- **Why:** People scan web pages. Long blocks of text get ignored.

### Rule 2: Active voice over passive
- **Good:** "We encrypt your data with AES-256"
- **Bad:** "Your data is encrypted with AES-256 by our system"
- **Test:** If you can add "by zombies" to the end of the sentence and it still makes grammatical sense, it's passive voice.

### Rule 3: "You" over "We"
- **Target:** 2:1 ratio of "you/your" to "we/our" across the full page
- **Count:** Tally every "you", "your", "yours", "yourself" vs. "we", "our", "ours", "us"
- **Flag:** If "we/our" count exceeds "you/your" count — the page is about the company, not the customer

### Rule 4: Benefits before features
- **Structure:** Lead with what the customer gets, then explain how
- **Good:** "Never miss a deadline again. Smart reminders ping you 24 hours before every due date."
- **Bad:** "Smart reminder system with configurable notification intervals and multi-channel delivery."

### Rule 5: One idea per section
- **Flag:** Sections that try to cover 3 benefits at once
- **Better:** Each section/card addresses one benefit with one supporting point

### Rule 6: Conversational tone
- **Test:** Read the copy out loud. If it sounds like a press release or legal document, rewrite it.
- **Flag:** "Leverage our cutting-edge solutions to optimize your workflow" (no one talks like this)
- **Better:** "Get your work done faster with tools that actually make sense"

## CTA Copy Rules

The words on the button matter more than you think.

### Rule 1: Action + value
- **Formula:** [Verb] + [what they get]
- **Good:** "Start my free trial", "Get the report", "Book a 15-min demo"
- **Bad:** "Submit", "Click here", "Continue", "Get started"

### Rule 2: First person works better
- **Good:** "Start my free trial" (I'm taking action)
- **Also good:** "Start your free trial" (you're being invited)
- **Bad:** "Start free trial" (impersonal, robotic)

### Rule 3: No friction words
- **Avoid:** "Buy", "Pay", "Commit", "Register" — these imply cost or effort
- **Prefer:** "Try", "Get", "Start", "Explore", "See" — these feel low-commitment
- **Exception:** Ecommerce can use "Add to cart" and "Buy now" because visitors expect it

### Rule 4: Match the offer
- **Flag:** Button says "Get started" but clicking leads to a "Contact sales" form
- **Flag:** Button says "Free trial" but the next page asks for a credit card
- **Rule:** What the button promises must match what the visitor gets

### Rule 5: Unique per page position
- **Flag:** Same CTA text repeated 5 times ("Get started" everywhere)
- **Better:** Vary the CTA text based on context:
  - After the hero: "Try it free"
  - After testimonials: "Join 10,000 happy teams"
  - After pricing: "Start my plan"

## Social Proof Copy Rules

How testimonials, metrics, and trust signals are written.

### Rule 1: Specific results over vague praise
- **Good:** "We reduced our onboarding time from 2 weeks to 3 days" - Sarah Chen, VP Engineering at Stripe
- **Bad:** "Great product! Highly recommend." - John D.
- **Flag:** Any testimonial without a specific outcome, name, or company

### Rule 2: Name, title, company, photo
- **Minimum:** Name + company/role
- **Better:** Name + title + company + photo
- **Best:** Name + title + company + photo + specific result
- **Flag:** Initials only, no photo, no company — feels fake

### Rule 3: Metrics need context
- **Good:** "12,000 teams trust Acme" (I can understand the scale)
- **Bad:** "Millions of data points processed" (so what? What does that mean for me?)
- **Flag:** Big numbers without context or relevance to the visitor

### Rule 4: Logos need recognition
- **Good:** Logos the target audience knows and respects
- **Bad:** 20 logos of companies nobody's heard of (creates visual noise, not trust)
- **Flag:** More than 8 logos in a row — diminishing returns, looks desperate

### Rule 5: "As seen in" must be real
- **Flag:** "As seen in Forbes" when it was actually a Forbes contributor post, not editorial
- **Flag:** Press logos from publications the target audience doesn't read

## Common Copy Sins (Always Flag)

These are red flags that hurt conversion regardless of context.

| Sin | Example | Why it's bad |
|-----|---------|-------------|
| **Buzzword bingo** | "Leverage our AI-powered, best-in-class, end-to-end solution" | Empty calories. Says nothing. Visitors tune out. |
| **"We" focused opening** | "We are a leading SaaS company founded in 2019..." | Nobody cares about you yet. Talk about the visitor first. |
| **Feature soup** | "Features: API access, SSO, webhooks, RBAC, SOC2..." | Features without context. What do these DO for the customer? |
| **Weasel words** | "Up to 10x faster", "As low as $9/mo", "Helps you..." | Hedge words undermine confidence. "Helps you save time" vs. "Saves you 4 hours/week" |
| **Passive voice everywhere** | "Workflows can be automated. Reports are generated." | Makes copy feel lifeless and evasive. Who does the automating? |
| **Wall of text** | Any paragraph over 5 lines | Visitors don't read on the web; they scan. Long paragraphs get skipped. |
| **"Click here" links** | "To learn more, click here" | Bad for accessibility (screen readers read "click here" out of context) and bad for SEO |
| **Exclamation overuse** | "Amazing features! Incredible results! Sign up today!" | One exclamation per page max. More feels like shouting. |
| **Fake urgency** | "Only 3 spots left!" (for a SaaS product with no capacity limit) | Destroys trust. Visitors see through it. |
| **"Solutions" without specifics** | "We provide solutions for your business challenges" | The most meaningless sentence in marketing. What solutions? What challenges? |
| **Superlatives without proof** | "The best CRM in the world", "The fastest platform" | Unsubstantiated claims. Back it up with a number or remove it. |
| **Double negatives** | "Don't miss out on not losing..." | Confusing. Rewrite in positive terms. |
| **Acronym overload** | "Our SaaS leverages ML, NLP, and RPA for B2B GTM" | If the visitor needs a glossary, the copy has failed. |

## Copy Scoring Quick Reference

When scoring the **Copy and Messaging** dimension:

| Quality | Score range | What it looks like |
|---------|------------|-------------------|
| Excellent | 85-100 | Clear headline, benefit-first, specific, "you" focused, scannable, StoryBrand narrative |
| Good | 70-84 | Mostly clear, some jargon, benefits present but not always leading, decent structure |
| Average | 55-69 | Vague headline, mixed features/benefits, some jargon, readable but not compelling |
| Weak | 40-54 | "We" focused, feature-heavy, jargon, long paragraphs, unclear value proposition |
| Poor | Below 40 | Can't understand what the product does, wall of text, buzzwords, no benefits, no structure |
