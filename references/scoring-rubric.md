# Scoring Rubric

Exact scoring criteria for each of the 6 audit dimensions. Every dimension is scored 0-100 using the checkpoints below. The overall score is a weighted average.

---

## 1. First Impression (Weight: 20%)

The "3-second test" — can a visitor understand what you do, who it's for, and what to do next within 3 seconds of landing?

| # | Checkpoint | Points | Pass | Fail |
|---|-----------|--------|------|------|
| 1 | Value proposition visible above the fold | 25 | A clear sentence explains what the product/service does and who it's for, visible without scrolling | No value prop above the fold, or it's buried below images/navigation/animations |
| 2 | Visual hierarchy guides the eye | 20 | The eye naturally flows: headline -> subhead -> CTA. One thing is clearly the biggest/boldest element. | Everything is the same size, or competing elements fight for attention. Eye doesn't know where to start. |
| 3 | Above-fold completeness | 20 | Above the fold contains: headline, subhead or supporting text, primary CTA, and at least one trust signal | Missing 2 or more of these elements above the fold |
| 4 | Professional appearance | 15 | Consistent colors, aligned elements, no visual clutter, appropriate whitespace, no broken images | Misaligned elements, clashing colors, cluttered layout, broken images, or amateur design |
| 5 | Differentiation | 10 | Something on the page signals what makes this different from alternatives (unique angle, specific number, unusual approach) | Could be any company in the space. Generic stock photo + generic headline. Nothing memorable. |
| 6 | Emotional first reaction | 10 | The page evokes a clear feeling: trust, excitement, curiosity, relief. The visitor feels "this is for me." | The page feels cold, confusing, or generic. No emotional signal. |

**Total: 100 points**

### Scoring notes
- If screenshots are available, score based on what's literally visible in the desktop viewport without scrolling.
- If text-only analysis, score based on DOM order (first ~500px worth of content).
- "Above the fold" means the initial viewport — roughly the first 800px of vertical space on desktop, 700px on mobile.

---

## 2. Copy and Messaging (Weight: 20%)

Is the writing clear, specific, benefit-focused, and free of jargon?

| # | Checkpoint | Points | Pass | Fail |
|---|-----------|--------|------|------|
| 1 | Headline clarity | 25 | The headline passes the "so what?" test — a stranger can understand what the product does and why it matters in one reading | Headline is vague ("Welcome to the future"), clever-but-unclear, or just the company name |
| 2 | Benefit-first language | 20 | Copy leads with what the customer gets (outcomes, feelings, results), not what the product has (features, specs, technology) | Copy is feature-first: "We use AI-powered neural networks" instead of "Find answers in seconds" |
| 3 | Jargon-free writing | 15 | A smart 14-year-old could understand every sentence. Industry terms are explained or avoided. | Uses insider jargon, acronyms, or technical terms the target customer wouldn't use in conversation |
| 4 | StoryBrand structure | 15 | The page follows a narrative: identifies the customer's problem, positions the product as the guide, shows a clear plan, and paints the success outcome | No story structure. Just a list of features or a wall of text with no emotional arc. |
| 5 | Specificity | 15 | Uses concrete numbers, timeframes, or outcomes: "Save 4 hours per week" instead of "Save time" | Vague claims: "boost productivity", "grow your business", "take it to the next level" |
| 6 | Scannable structure | 10 | Short paragraphs (3 lines max), clear section headers, bullet points for lists, bold for key phrases | Long unbroken paragraphs, no headers, no visual breaks. Wall-of-text effect. |

**Total: 100 points**

### Scoring notes
- Read ALL copy on the page, not just above the fold.
- "Jargon" depends on the target audience. B2B SaaS can use some industry terms their buyers know. But "synergize your cross-functional workflows" is always jargon.
- StoryBrand doesn't need to be explicitly labeled — just check if the narrative elements are present.

---

## 3. Call-to-Action (Weight: 15%)

Is the CTA visible, compelling, and the clear next step?

| # | Checkpoint | Points | Pass | Fail |
|---|-----------|--------|------|------|
| 1 | CTA visibility | 25 | The primary CTA button is immediately noticeable — high contrast, generous size, stands out from everything else on the page | CTA blends into the background, is too small, or looks like a text link instead of a button |
| 2 | Button copy | 25 | CTA text is specific and value-oriented: "Start free trial", "Get my report", "Book a 15-min demo" | Generic text: "Submit", "Click here", "Learn more", "Get started" (with no context of what "started" means) |
| 3 | Single primary focus | 20 | One clear primary CTA dominates the page. Secondary actions (like "watch demo") are visually subordinate. | Multiple CTAs compete for attention at the same level. Visitor doesn't know which to click. |
| 4 | Contrast and design | 15 | CTA button color contrasts with the page background AND with surrounding elements. Button has padding, clear borders or shadow, and looks clickable. | CTA is same color as other elements, has no contrast with background, or doesn't look like a button |
| 5 | Placement above fold | 15 | At least one CTA is visible above the fold without scrolling. Additional CTAs appear after key content sections. | No CTA above the fold. Visitor must scroll to find how to take action. |

**Total: 100 points**

### Scoring notes
- "Primary CTA" = the one action the business most wants visitors to take.
- Navigation links ("About", "Pricing") are NOT CTAs for scoring purposes.
- A sticky header CTA counts as "above the fold" only if it's visually prominent (not a tiny text link).
- Ghost buttons (outline only) as the PRIMARY CTA lose 10 points on visibility.

## 4. Trust and Social Proof (Weight: 15%)

Does the page give visitors enough reason to trust the business?

| # | Checkpoint | Points | Pass | Fail |
|---|-----------|--------|------|------|
| 1 | Testimonials or reviews | 25 | Real testimonials with names, photos, or company names. Specific results mentioned ("grew revenue 40%"). | No testimonials, or obviously fake ones ("Great product! - John D.") with no specifics |
| 2 | Logos or credibility badges | 20 | "As seen in" media logos, partner logos, certification badges, or "trusted by [brand names]" | No logos, no badges, no external validation of any kind |
| 3 | Numbers and metrics | 20 | Specific user counts, results, or scale: "12,000 teams", "99.9% uptime", "$2.4M saved" | No numbers anywhere. No indication of traction, scale, or results. |
| 4 | Risk reversal | 20 | Free trial, money-back guarantee, "cancel anytime", free tier, or demo — something that removes the risk of trying | No risk reversal. Visitor must commit (pay, sign contract) with no safety net. |
| 5 | Objection handling | 15 | FAQ section, comparison table, "how it works" section, or direct addressing of common concerns | No FAQ, no "how it works", no attempt to answer the visitor's unspoken "but what about..." questions |

**Total: 100 points**

### Scoring notes
- Quality matters more than quantity. One detailed testimonial with a name and photo beats five anonymous one-liners.
- For early-stage products with no customers yet, partial credit for: founder credentials, beta user count, relevant experience, or institutional backing.
- "Risk reversal" adjusts by business type — a free newsletter signup is inherently low-risk, so this checkpoint is easier to pass.

## 5. Mobile and Accessibility (Weight: 15%)

Does the page work on phones and is it accessible to people with disabilities?

| # | Checkpoint | Points | Pass | Fail |
|---|-----------|--------|------|------|
| 1 | Responsive layout | 20 | Page looks intentionally designed for mobile — not just a squished desktop version. No horizontal scrolling. Content reflows properly. | Broken layout on mobile, horizontal scroll, overlapping elements, or content cut off |
| 2 | Touch targets | 15 | Buttons and links are at least 44x44 pixels with adequate spacing between them. Easy to tap without accidentally hitting the wrong thing. | Tiny buttons, links too close together, or interactive elements smaller than 44x44px |
| 3 | Font size | 15 | Body text is at least 16px on mobile. Headings are proportionally larger. Nothing requires zooming to read. | Body text below 16px on mobile. Users need to pinch-zoom to read. |
| 4 | Color contrast (WCAG AA) | 15 | Text has at least 4.5:1 contrast ratio against its background. Large text (18px+ bold or 24px+) meets 3:1. | Light gray text on white background, or any text that's hard to read due to low contrast |
| 5 | Image alt text | 10 | Meaningful images have descriptive alt text. Decorative images have empty alt (alt=""). No missing alt attributes. | Images have no alt text, or all images use the filename as alt text |
| 6 | Keyboard navigation | 10 | All interactive elements are reachable with Tab key. Focus order makes sense. Focus indicators are visible. | Can't tab to buttons, focus indicators removed (outline:none with no replacement), or skip-nav is missing |
| 7 | Heading hierarchy | 8 | One H1, logical heading order (H1 -> H2 -> H3), no skipped levels. Screen readers can navigate by headings. | Multiple H1s, skipped heading levels (H1 -> H3), or headings used for styling instead of structure |
| 8 | Color not sole indicator | 7 | Information isn't conveyed by color alone. Error states use icons or text in addition to red. Links are underlined or otherwise distinguished. | Links only distinguishable by color. Error messages only indicated by red text with no icon or label. |

**Total: 100 points**

### Scoring notes
- Mobile testing requires screenshots at 375px viewport (iPhone SE) or actual device testing.
- If no screenshot tool is available, check CSS for responsive breakpoints and flag any fixed-width elements.
- Contrast can be checked from CSS values even without screenshots.
- Keyboard navigation requires Playwright or manual testing. If unavailable, check for `outline:none` in CSS and flag as a concern.

## 6. Performance and Technical (Weight: 15%)

Does the page load fast and cover technical basics?

| # | Checkpoint | Points | Pass | Fail |
|---|-----------|--------|------|------|
| 1 | Page load time | 25 | Page loads in under 3 seconds on a standard connection. Above-fold content is visible within 1.5 seconds (LCP). | Page takes more than 3 seconds to load. Visitors see a blank screen or loading spinner for too long. |
| 2 | SEO basics — title and meta | 20 | Page has a unique title tag (under 60 chars), meta description (under 160 chars), and OG tags for social sharing | Missing title tag, missing meta description, or title is just the company name with no context |
| 3 | SEO basics — headings and structure | 15 | Proper H1 tag present, semantic HTML used (header, main, footer, nav), clean URL structure | No H1 tag, all divs with no semantic HTML, or URL has random query parameters |
| 4 | No broken links or images | 15 | All links lead somewhere. All images load. No 404s. | Broken links, missing images, or links to placeholder pages (#, javascript:void) |
| 5 | No console errors | 10 | Browser console shows no JavaScript errors. No failed network requests (except third-party tracking). | JavaScript errors in console, failed API calls, or errors that could affect functionality |
| 6 | HTTPS | 10 | Page loads over HTTPS with a valid certificate. No mixed content warnings. | HTTP only, expired certificate, or mixed content (HTTPS page loading HTTP resources) |
| 7 | No render-blocking issues | 5 | No massive unoptimized images (over 1MB), no render-blocking scripts that delay the page from appearing | Huge unoptimized images, multiple render-blocking scripts in the head, or visible layout shift after load |

**Total: 100 points**

### Scoring notes
- If Lighthouse/Chrome DevTools is available, use actual metrics for load time and Core Web Vitals.
- If no performance tools are available, estimate based on: page size (check response size), number of external resources (scripts, stylesheets, fonts), and image sizes visible in the HTML.
- Console error checking requires Playwright or Chrome DevTools. If unavailable, note "could not verify — install Playwright for console error detection."
- HTTPS can be checked from the URL alone.
- If the page returns a 5xx status code, checkpoint 1 (Page load time) scores 0 automatically. Checkpoint 4 (No broken links) also scores 0. Other checkpoints may be unverifiable — score them as 0 with a note.
- PWA detection: If the page has a valid `manifest.json` and service worker registration, note as a positive signal in the findings (+5 bonus on Performance, max 100). PWA capability shows technical sophistication.

---

## Overall Score Calculation

```
Overall = (First Impression x 0.20) + (Copy x 0.20) + (CTA x 0.15) + (Trust x 0.15) + (Mobile x 0.15) + (Performance x 0.15)
```

### Grade Scale

| Grade | Score | Meaning |
|-------|-------|---------|
| A+ | 95-100 | Excellent. Minor tweaks only. |
| A | 90-94 | Very strong. A few small improvements possible. |
| B+ | 85-89 | Good page with clear room to improve. |
| B | 80-84 | Solid foundation, missing some key elements. |
| C+ | 75-79 | Average. Several important things need fixing. |
| C | 70-74 | Below average. Visitors are probably leaving confused. |
| D | 60-69 | Needs serious work. Major issues with clarity or trust. |
| F | Below 60 | Actively hurting your business. Fix urgently. |

### Anti-Pattern Penalties

Some issues from `anti-patterns.md` trigger automatic point deductions on the relevant dimension:

- `outline:none` without replacement: -10 on Mobile and Accessibility
- Auto-playing video with sound: -15 on First Impression
- Popup/modal on page load: -10 on First Impression
- "Click here" as link text: -5 on Copy and Messaging (per occurrence, max -15)
- No HTTPS: -10 on Performance and Technical (full checkpoint fails)
- Images without width/height causing layout shift: -5 on Performance and Technical
